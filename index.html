Work Reimagined — SDG&E Contact Centre

From Current Roles to the New Workforce

Designing the future of work in an agentic gas utility contact centre.

Sankey & Ledger

The Core Question

SDG&E's contact centre handles millions of contacts a year — the majority of which follow predictable, structured patterns: bill queries, start/stop service, outage status, payment arrangements. Agentic AI can contain most of this volume autonomously.

The methodology starts with the work itself, not the technology. What does the contact centre actually do — and how much judgment, regulatory accountability, or human empathy does each activity genuinely require?

Why Gas Utility is Different

Unlike most contact centres, a gas utility operates under CPUC regulation and DOT Part 192 safety obligations. Emergency calls — gas leaks, carbon monoxide, pressure anomalies — are non-delegable by law. Medical Baseline customers carry legal protection thresholds. Hardship disconnections carry political and reputational risk.

The redesign must honour these constraints. Not every activity can be automated, and the ones that cannot are often the most consequential.

Assign each activity an automation archetype. Four patterns cover the full range: Deterministic (rules-based, zero judgment), Agentic AI (reasoning, multi-step, tool-using), Human + AI Augmented (AI proposes, human decides), and Human-led (judgment, regulatory accountability, safety). CPUC and DOT Part 192 obligations constrain archetype assignment on safety-critical activities.
03 →
Map activities to future state targets. Each activity flows to either a future human role (redefined around judgment and accountability) or an AI system (purpose-built for a specific task domain). Agentic and Augmented activities split across both — 70% to AI systems, 30% to human oversight roles.
04 →
Derive the new SDG&E workforce model. Future human roles are purpose-built for what AI cannot own — emergency response, financial hardship judgment, Medical Baseline accountability, commercial specialist decisions. The result is a smaller, more senior human team working alongside capable, gas-utility-specific AI agents.
Deterministic
Rules-based, high-volume, structured. Defined inputs and outputs. No judgment required. Maps entirely to AI systems.
Agentic AI
Reasoning across context, tools, and data to produce outputs. Multi-step. 70% flows to AI systems; 30% to human oversight roles.
Human + AI Augmented
AI produces the first draft, proposes the next step, or assembles the evidence. Human decides, adjusts, and owns the outcome.
Human-led
Judgment, accountability, empathy. Vulnerable customers, policy exceptions, regulatory decisions. Cannot be delegated to a model.

"The goal is not automating gas utility contact centre roles. It is redesigning the contact centre around what AI can fully own, what it can prepare for a human, and what only a human can ever answer for — especially when safety, vulnerability, or regulatory accountability is on the line."

SDG&E / Element Energy — Agentic AI Vision Video Transcript

What the Transcript Tells Us

The video frames SDG&E's 2030 ambition as AI-to-AI agent commerce — a customer's personal AI agent negotiates with SDG&E's utility AI agent, handling a full move-in autonomously: energy preferences, package personalisation, EV charger installation, and battery storage activation, all without the customer lifting a finger beyond initial consent.

This is not an incremental upgrade to the contact centre. It is a fundamentally different service model built on three capabilities SDG&E does not yet have: AI-to-AI authentication protocols, life-event detection from external signals, and personalised energy package assembly across full home profile data.

The Interim as Foundation, Not Compromise

The near-term programme (2026–2028) builds the infrastructure the 2030 vision requires. Containment of high-volume contacts through the Service Transactions Engine and Outage Agent establishes AI reliability at scale. Payment Agent deployment builds trust in AI-led financial conversations. Quality scoring builds the regulatory evidence base for AI compliance.

Each interim deployment is not a lesser version of the vision — it is a proof point that earns the organisational and regulatory permission to take the next step. The transcript's "simple, personalized, seamless" principle applies at every stage.
